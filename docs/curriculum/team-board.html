Who is best for what

A decision aid for assigning hackathon roles. It shows its reasoning and its close alternatives. You make the call.

Privacy. This page stores names and ratings of students, many of whom are minors. Everything stays in this browser's local storage. Nothing is sent anywhere. Do not commit exported files to the repository, and clear the data when the program ends.
01 — People

What you observed, and what they want

Rate each signal from 1 to 5 using your notes from Weeks 1 to 7. Leave a cell blank if you have no evidence: it counts as a neutral 3 and is flagged. Students rank the five roles on their slip, 1 for the one they are most curious about.

Signals: Decides cuts scope and makes the call. Interface screens, flow and critique. Technical reads and debugs AI output and code. Reliable Git, PRs, deploys, finishes on time. Story pitch, README, video. Roles: PL Product Lead, IB Interface Builder, AI AI Engineer, PC Platform Captain, DP Demo Producer.

02 — Suggestions

The best fit for each team

Teams of 3 to 5 people. Smaller teams combine roles. Read the flags and the alternatives before you accept anything.

03 — Settings

The weights are a judgment. Change them.

The defaults are a starting guess, not a fact about people. If the output feels wrong, the weights are the first place to look.

How much does "want" count?

0% means only what you observed. 100% means only what they ranked. The Week 7 slide promises 50%.

50%

Teams of 3 combine Product Lead + Demo Producer, and AI Engineer + Platform Captain. Teams of 4 combine Product Lead + Demo Producer.

What each role needs from the signals

Each row is normalised, so only the proportions matter. A row of zeros is ignored for that role (treated as neutral).

How the score works

For one person and one role: fit = (1 - w) x observed + w x want, on a scale of 1 to 5.

observed is the weighted average of that person's five signals, using the role's row in the table above. A missing rating counts as 3. want is 5 for the role they ranked first, down to 1 for the one they ranked last. An unranked role counts as 3.

For a team, the board tries every way of giving each person one job and adds the fits. It shows the best arrangement and the next two. If the top two are close, the choice is yours, and it says so.

What it cannot know: who works well together, who is shy, who is having a bad month. Those matter more than a point of fit. Use the board to check your own thinking, then explain your decision to the team.
